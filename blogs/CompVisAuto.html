Computer Vision for Autonomous Vehicles: Principles, Techniques, and Applications

By Jyothish • Jatani, Odisha • Monday, 08 December 2025

Abstract (60 words): Computer vision enables autonomous vehicles to perceive and interpret their surroundings. This article explains core principles, algorithms for detection and tracking, sensor fusion, and challenges such as weather and occlusion. It compares computer vision with LiDAR and radar, and maps applications across navigation, safety, and industry, with design considerations and references for reproducible deployment.

Introduction

Autonomous vehicles rely heavily on computer vision to understand their environment. Cameras capture visual data, which is processed by algorithms to detect lanes, traffic signs, pedestrians, and other vehicles. Combined with sensor fusion, computer vision provides critical input for navigation, safety, and decision-making.

Key idea: Computer vision transforms raw camera data into actionable insights for autonomous driving.

Core Techniques

Lane Detection

Algorithms identify lane markings using edge detection, Hough transforms, or deep learning, enabling lane keeping and path planning.

Object Detection

Models like YOLO, Faster R-CNN, and SSD detect vehicles, pedestrians, and obstacles in real time.

Traffic Sign Recognition

CNN-based classifiers recognize speed limits, stop signs, and signals, ensuring compliance with road rules.

Semantic Segmentation

Pixel-level classification separates road, sidewalk, vehicles, and vegetation, providing detailed scene understanding.

Sensor Fusion

Vision data is fused with LiDAR, radar, and GNSS to improve robustness under challenging conditions.

Challenges

  • Weather: Rain, fog, and snow degrade camera performance.
  • Lighting: Low light or glare affects detection accuracy.
  • Occlusion: Objects hidden behind others complicate recognition.
  • Real-time constraints: Algorithms must run with low latency for safety-critical decisions.

Comparison with Other Sensors

Computer vision provides rich semantic information but struggles in adverse conditions. LiDAR offers precise depth but is costly. Radar excels in detecting objects in poor weather but lacks detail. Sensor fusion combines strengths, ensuring reliable perception.

Applications

Navigation

Vision-based lane detection and mapping guide autonomous vehicles along roads and highways.

Safety

Real-time pedestrian and vehicle detection prevent collisions and enhance driver assistance systems.

Industry

Computer vision supports fleet management, logistics, and smart city integration of autonomous vehicles.

Design Considerations

  • Dataset Quality: Diverse training data improves robustness across environments.
  • Algorithm Efficiency: Optimize models for embedded hardware with limited resources.
  • Safety Standards: Ensure compliance with ISO 26262 and automotive safety regulations.
  • Redundancy: Combine multiple sensors to mitigate failures.
